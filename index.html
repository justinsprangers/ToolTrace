Scale reference

Drag the 4 handles onto the paper corners if they're off. Scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan.

“Detect grid” finds the mat lines and fills in the handles and square counts. If it can't, put the handles on 4 grid crossings and type how many squares are between them. The handles snap to the nearest crossings. Measure the size of the smallest squares on your mat.

Trace

Picks up shadows or grid lines → raise Sensitivity or lower Brightness weight. Misses shiny or pale parts → lower Sensitivity. Tools merging → lower Bridge gaps.

Fix up

Tools above the Trace view: Brush keep / erase (B / E) add or remove exactly what you paint, e.g. the mat between snip handles. [ and ] change the brush size; Ctrl+Z undoes. Select (V) is for shapes and nodes, below.

Shapes

Untick a shape to leave it out. Click one (here or on the picture) to select it and edit its nodes.

Outline settings for all shapes

Corners (cyan) stay sharp. Smoothing only removes noise between them; straight runs become exact lines and curves stay curves. Node detail sets how closely the nodes follow the traced edge.

Shape

The two halves are averaged across the pink axis and the nodes mirrored, so the outline is exactly symmetric. Editing a node mirrors it to the other side when you let go.

Nodes: drag to move. Click the outline to add a node. Double-click a node (or C) to switch corner ■ / smooth ●. L makes the segment after the selected node a straight line. Delete removes the selected node, or excludes the shape if no node is selected. Esc deselects.

Tips for a good photo
  • Plain white paper is the most reliable reference. Use tabloid paper or poster board for long tools.
  • Shoot straight down from high up with 2–3× zoom. Close wide-angle shots make tall parts look bigger.
  • Even light, no hard shadows. Spread tools out so they don't touch.
  • Several tools in one photo is fine: each becomes its own shape.

Selected

Drag to move, drag the round handle to rotate (hold Shift to snap 15°). Arrow keys nudge, R rotates, Delete removes.

Finger notches

Pick Notch (N) above the picture. A dashed circle shows where it will go. Near a tool it snaps onto the outline (hold Alt to place freely), belongs to that tool (moves and rotates with it), and is never deeper than the tool. If the tool has a symmetry axis, a mirrored twin is added. Click a notch to remove it (and its twin); drag notches in Select mode.
